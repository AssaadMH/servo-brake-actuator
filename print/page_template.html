Twin-cable brake drum

A printed drum that sits on a TD-8130MG servo and pulls two bicycle brake cables at once, equally. One turn of the servo, 70 mm of cable. The stock star horn is swallowed inside it, so the servo's factory spline takes the torque and no printed plastic ever touches the shaft.

Rev B — the slicer caught two things I got wrong

Square grooves became 90° V-sheaves. A square channel left a 3 mm ledge hanging in mid-air above each groove, all the way around — the slicer called it a collapsing overhang and it would have drooped. V-walls at 45° hold themselves up, and the cable now self-centres at exactly the design radius.

The centre bore grew to Ø14.5 and runs straight through. The old Ø9 bore left a thin unsupported ring at the pocket ceiling with nothing under it to print onto. Matching the bore to the horn boss removes the ring entirely.

Both parts now slice with zero support warnings.

01 The part

Two stacked V-sheaves, two nipple pockets 180° apart, four retaining slots, and a star-shaped pocket underneath. Mesh verified watertight, single shell, zero non-manifold edges.

02 Section through the cable plane

Cut vertically through both nipple pockets. Note the asymmetry — it is deliberate. The right-hand nipple feeds the lower groove, the left-hand one feeds the upper groove, so the two cable tensions sit on opposite sides of the shaft and cancel instead of adding.

  1. 1Nipple pocket, Ø6.6. Drop the barrel nipple in from the top face; it seats at groove height.
  2. 2Wire slot into the lower V. The wire leaves the nipple sideways. The red dot is where it seats — the V is exactly cable-width at Ø44.56, so the drum sizes itself.
  3. 3Second pocket, opposite side. Shallower, because it feeds the upper V.
  4. 4Ø14.5 bore, straight through. Same size as the horn boss. A screwdriver reaches the servo's centre screw after assembly.
  5. 5Horn pocket, 4.4 deep — dashed, because the arms sit at 45° and are behind this cut. Four retaining slots follow the same 45°.

03 Stroke sets the force

This is the whole trade, and it is worth understanding before printing. The drum diameter is fixed entirely by how far you want the cable to travel. Bigger drum, longer pull, weaker pull — there is no way around it short of a stronger servo or gearing.

If it turns out weak

Change one number. Halving STROKE in the .scad halves the drum and doubles the pull. Nothing else in the model needs touching — every dimension is derived from it.

04 Assembly order

The order matters: the horn has to be on the servo before the drum goes over it, and the cables load last.

  1. Print the fit coupon and check it on your horn.

    Five minutes. It should push on snug with no rotational play — slop here becomes brake slop later. If it is wrong, measure the horn and correct the four dimensions at the top of the .scad.

  2. Screw the stock star horn onto the servo.

    Its factory centre screw, normal torque. This is the joint that carries all 30 kg·cm.

  3. Drop the drum over the horn.

    The star pocket underneath swallows it. The pocket is rotated 45° from the nipple bores so nothing collides.

  4. Retain it with M3 screws through the top slots.

    Two is enough, four is better. The slots run r = 8…19 mm, so whichever holes your horn has will line up with something.

  5. Load the two cables.

    Nipple down its hole from the top face, wire out through the side slot into its groove. Wind both the same direction.

Not designed yet — you still need this

The drum only pulls the inner wire. The outer housing must be anchored to the aluminum plate at a fixed point, aimed tangentially at each groove level. Without those two stops the housing just compresses and nothing moves.

  • Z tops out at 19.55 mm, matching the model
  • Absolute E (M82), matching the N2's firmware
  • No G29 — your N2 is levelled by hand, a probe command would crash it
  • No support material and no overhang warnings from the slicer

Watch the first layer

The start and end G-code is generic Marlin, not Raise3D's official ideaMaker profile. It homes, heats, and lays a prime line along the front edge. Stay with it for the first layer the first time you run it.

PLA caveat

PLA creeps under sustained load and softens near 55 °C. If this holds the brake applied for long stretches, or lives in a closed enclosure, re-slice in PETG — same geometry.
