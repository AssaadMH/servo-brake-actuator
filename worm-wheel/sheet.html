Helix — from the measured worm

Worm measured: single start, 10.00 mm outside diameter. The lead angle is defined on the worm's pitch diameter, not its outside diameter, and it uses the axial module: 

d1    = worm_OD - 2·m_x
      = 10.00 - 1.60      = 8.40 mm

helix = atan(starts · m_x / d1)
      = atan(0.8 / 8.4)   = 5.4403°
Correction

I earlier gave 4.6° for a 10 mm single-start worm. That was wrong — it used the worm's outside diameter where the pitch diameter belongs, and the normal module where the axial one belongs. Both errors bias the angle low. The correct figure is 5.4403°, 0.87° higher, and it is what the shipped model is cut to.

Clamp anatomy

Ø7.10 Gripping bore

The body bore is 0.10 looser at Ø7.20 so the gear slides on freely and the hub alone does the clamping.

1.20 → r 18.5 Slit

Stops well inside the tooth roots at r 27.6, so the gear stays a complete unbroken 72-tooth ring — unlike a split pulley, which runs its slit out through the rim.

Ø3.00 Root relief

The slit ends in a rounded relief rather than a sharp corner. On a printed part that corner is the crack starter.

M3 · Ø6.40 c'bore Pinch bolt

Seats land on flat parallel pads on the ear, not on the curved hub — a plain hull would still be on its tangent there and leave a skin over the counterbore.

Ø4.00 × 5.0 Insert seat

M3 brass heat-set in the far jaw. Stops 0.9 short of the slit so it stays fully captive in one jaw.

teeth down Print orientation

Not just about supports: the C-section flexes circumferentially, so stress runs along the layers instead of peeling them apart.

Before you print

RH or LH? Check the worm's hand

A right-hand wheel will not mesh with a left-hand worm. Look at the worm side-on: threads climbing left-to-right, like an ordinary bolt, means right hand. Most automotive worms are — but check, don't assume. Both files are built.

not throated Cylindrical, not globoid

A proper worm wheel is hollowed to wrap the worm, giving line contact. This meets it on a short patch instead. It matches what your original appears to be, and a throated form isn't printable at 0.8 module — but expect a life below the moulded part's.

spur first If you'd rather not gamble

The straight-cut build turns against either hand, just noisily. Print it as a test fit, confirm the mesh and the clamp, then commit to the helical one.
